### Keep bases visible before endings

Three words preserve useful related forms. *Action* keeps the base **act** before **ion**. *Suddenly* keeps **sudden**, including double **d**, before **ly**. *Natural* is related to **nature**; the final **e** of *nature* is dropped before **al**, leaving **natur + al**. Each relationship accounts for letters that may be harder to recover by sound alone.

Other words are best organized with stable written syllables. *Process* can be held as **proc-ess**, with one **c** and double **s**. *Problem* uses **prob-lem**, and *provide* uses **pro-vide**, finishing with silent **e**. *Present* keeps the same spelling whether it names a gift, describes something that is here, or means to show something to others.

### Protect the exact vowel and consonant sequences

Several spellings depend on an exact letter sequence. *Piece* uses **ie** before **ce**, while *effect* begins with **e** and has double **f**. Meaning helps select each of these from a sound-alike or commonly confused spelling, so their notes include short contexts.

The remaining notes preserve details that unstressed speech can blur: **a** in **com-pa-ny**, the opening **su** and double **p** in *suppose*, and every vowel in **ex-per-i-ence**. Straightforward parts remain straightforward; the notes focus only on letters for which a structural anchor adds useful support.
