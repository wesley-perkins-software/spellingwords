One of the trickier discoveries in English spelling is that ER, IR, and UR can all make the same r-controlled vowel sound — the one heard in *her*, *bird*, and *turn*. But while the sounds match, the spellings do not: each word has its own correct spelling, and they are not interchangeable. Children learn which letters belong to which word, and that knowledge comes from meeting each word many times.

This list gathers ten common words across all three patterns. Seeing *girl*, *sister*, *hurt*, and *winter* together helps children begin to notice which spelling belongs to each familiar word, building the kind of visual memory that makes r-controlled vowels click.
