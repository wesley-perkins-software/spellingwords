# Source Map

## Purpose

This document maps educational vocabulary sources to their coverage status in the spellingwords word universe. It answers:

- Which sources are currently represented?
- Which sources have been partially covered?
- Which sources are candidates for future research?
- Where are the known gaps?
- What should we examine next?

This is a planning and editorial reference, not a repository of source content. It complements `WORD_UNIVERSE.md` (strategy and quality standards) and `WORD_CATALOG.md` (current inventory).

---

## Source Use Policy

Quality and traceability matter more than volume. The safe research workflow:

1. Identify a source and determine whether it is public, canonical, or proprietary.
2. Do not copy source content directly into this codebase.
3. Use the source to identify coverage gaps and confirm word appropriateness.
4. Write original sentences manually — no AI-generated sentences.
5. Add words only after editorial review and sentence assignment.
6. Record source influence at a high level only (this document).

Proprietary commercial curricula may be referenced as research guides only. Their word lists may not be reproduced here.

## Current Source Coverage

### Reading Rockets Basic Spelling Vocabulary

**Status:** In progress — primary influence on the sentence bank.

Reading Rockets publishes a research-based basic spelling vocabulary organized by grade level (K–5). The current sentence bank was heavily influenced by this source, particularly at the K–3 level. It serves as the primary grade-level vocabulary anchor for the word universe.

Known follow-up work:

- Formally audit grade-by-grade coverage against the current catalog.
- Identify which grade 4–5 words from this source are absent.
- Upper-elementary gaps are the highest expansion priority (grade 4–5 is currently 9% of the universe).

The full Reading Rockets list should be reviewed independently; it is not reproduced here.

### Dolch Sight Words

**Status:** Complete — well represented through existing sight-word lists.

The Dolch list is a canonical source of high-frequency sight words across pre-K through grade 3. The app's existing sight-word curated lists draw directly from Dolch groupings. Coverage is considered strong at K–2; grade 3 Dolch words should be confirmed in the catalog.

The full Dolch list is not reproduced here.

### Existing Phonics Collections

**Status:** Strong — represented through existing curated lists.

The app includes phonics-pattern lists covering:

- CVC (consonant-vowel-consonant) words
- Silent E patterns
- Vowel teams (ai, ea, oa, etc.)
- R-controlled vowels (ar, er, ir, or, ur)

These collections reflect standard early-literacy phonics scope and sequence. Coverage for K–2 phonics patterns is considered solid. Advanced phonics patterns (consonant clusters, multisyllabic decoding, less common vowel teams) may have gaps.

## Future Source Candidates

### Fry Words

**Status:** Not started.

The Fry Instant Word list is a high-frequency word source often used alongside or instead of Dolch, extending into grade 5. It is useful for:

- Identifying high-frequency words not yet in the catalog
- Confirming grade 3–5 coverage
- SEO relevance (Fry words are commonly searched by educators)

The Fry list is widely published in public educational contexts. Coverage should be compared against the current catalog to find gaps. The list itself is not reproduced here.

### UFLI (University of Florida Literacy Institute)

**Status:** Not started.

UFLI provides a structured, research-based phonics scope and sequence that is publicly available and widely used in structured literacy programs. It is most useful for:

- Auditing phonics-pattern coverage and sequence
- Ensuring phonics lists follow a logical progression
- Identifying underrepresented phonics patterns

UFLI should inform list organization and sequencing, not serve as a raw word source. The scope-and-sequence framework, not its word examples, is the editorial value.

### Wonders (McGraw-Hill)

**Status:** Future candidate — proprietary.

Wonders is a widely adopted commercial reading and spelling curriculum. It may be useful as a research reference to understand what words students encounter in school. However, its word lists are proprietary and may not be copied or adapted. Any editorial influence should be limited to confirming that common school words are present in our universe — not importing Wonders content directly.

### Benchmark Advance (Benchmark Education)

**Status:** Future candidate — proprietary.

Benchmark Advance is another commonly adopted commercial curriculum. The same caution applies as with Wonders: reference only, no content reproduction. Useful to confirm real-world classroom relevance.

## Underdeveloped Vocabulary Areas

### Grade 4–5 Vocabulary

The highest-priority gap. The word universe currently contains only 81 grade 4–5 words (9% of the total). This tier needs vocabulary that is curriculum-aligned, not just phonetically harder. Reading Rockets and Fry are the recommended starting points for expansion here.

### Morphology / Roots / Prefixes / Suffixes

Not formally addressed. Common Latin and Greek roots, prefixes, and suffixes are broadly in the public domain and appropriate to use as editorial guides. However, morphology lists need manual curation — word families should be selected by a human editor who verifies spelling complexity, grade fit, and sentence quality. No morphology lists should be created until the grade 4–5 vocabulary gap is partially resolved.

### Academic Vocabulary

Science vocabulary, social studies vocabulary, and writing vocabulary are not yet represented. These are appropriate future categories for grade 3–5 expansion, but no word lists should be added until the general word universe is more complete.

### Challenge Words

Upper-elementary and above words are sparse. These words may draw from a broader range of sources but require the same editorial care: original sentences, grade assignment, and review-word audit before publishing.

## Sources We Intentionally Avoid

The following sources are not used, regardless of content volume:

- **AI-generated word lists** — no provenance, unverifiable grade appropriateness, inconsistent quality
- **Random worksheet sites** — unattributed, often inaccurate grade labels, no editorial standard
- **Unattributed blogs** — no accountability, frequently derived from other unattributed sources
- **SEO list farms** — optimized for search traffic, not learning outcomes
- **Proprietary content copied from commercial curricula** — legally and editorially inappropriate

Quality and traceability matter more than volume. Every word in the universe must have a hand-written sentence, a grade assignment, and an identifiable rationale for inclusion.
