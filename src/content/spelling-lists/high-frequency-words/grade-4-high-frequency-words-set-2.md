### Build from familiar words and a related base

Two words join familiar words without changing their spellings: *within* is **with + in**, and *however* is **how + ever**. The word *written* uses a different relationship. Connecting it to **write** explains its meaning and most of its letters, while its note captures the final-**e** change and doubled **t**.

*Common* also needs a doubled consonant: divide it as **com-mon** and keep **mm** at the syllable boundary. In *include*, the written syllables **in-clude** provide a reliable structure; they are useful spelling chunks here without needing a broader lesson about the word's history.

### Sort the stable vowel spellings

Several words use vowel spellings that stay reliable on the page even when sound alone does not identify every letter. *Reason* uses **ea** before **son**, and *course* uses **our** before **se**. *Since* ends with **ce**, with silent **e** following the **s** sound represented by **c**. The notes give more focused attention to **ure** in *sure* and **ough** in *though*.

Meaning is part of retrieving two spellings. *Whether* needs **wh** and differs from *weather*; *whole* also needs **wh** and differs from *hole*. Their context examples identify the intended word without turning the page into a general lesson on homophones.
