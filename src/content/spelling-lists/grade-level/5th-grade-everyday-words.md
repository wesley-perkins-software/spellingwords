By fifth grade, students are expected to write extended responses, take notes, and communicate clearly across every subject. Words like *although* (with that silent-gh borrowed from Old English) and *journey* (with its unexpected -ey ending) trip up even strong readers who have never had to spell them from memory.

Words like *consider* and *improve* show up in teacher feedback and assignment prompts as often as they do in student writing — making them worth practicing directly. Spelling these eleven words automatically lets a fifth grader stay focused on the quality of their ideas, not the letters.
