First Words showed that sounding out works. This step slows down and stays with a single vowel sound — the short "a" heard in the middle of *hat* and *map* — so your child can hear it clearly, over and over, before the progression moves to a new vowel.

Every word here uses a simple consonant-vowel-consonant (CVC) pattern, with no silent letters, blends, or digraphs. The beginning and ending consonants change while **a** stays in the middle, helping a child notice which part of the spelling carries the target vowel sound.

### How to practice this list

Say a word without showing it and have your child repeat it, stretch its three sounds—/h/, /a/, /t/—and write **h-a-t**. Then uncover the word and ask the child to check the beginning, middle, and ending. Practice a few related words first, then mix their order so the middle **a** must be heard rather than predicted from the list.

Leaving out a consonant is different from changing the vowel. For a missing beginning or ending, stretch that edge of the word; if the child writes another vowel, compare the attempt with a known word such as *hat* and listen again to the middle sound. Calm correction is part of the practice, not a failed turn.

When a child can spell the words in mixed order with growing independence, keep **a** in the middle as the consonants change, and correct an occasional mistake after comparing it with a known example, they're ready for Kindergarten Short I Words.
