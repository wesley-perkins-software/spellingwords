### Compare what silent e does

The words *more*, *love*, and *name* all end with silent **e**. In *name*, it makes the **a** say its name. In *more*, the vowel works with **r** before the silent **e**. In *love*, the vowel does not become long, so that word needs separate attention.

### Use vowel teams and sturdy consonant endings

The words *may* and *need* use familiar long-vowel teams: **ay** at the end of *may* and **ee** in *need*. Several other words can be secured by listening through their endings: **lp** in *help*, **st** in *most* and *last*, **ng** in *long*, and **ll** in *still*.

The word *than* has a familiar short-vowel pattern after **th**. Most of its spelling can be built from those known sounds, while *put* needs a more specific vowel connection.
