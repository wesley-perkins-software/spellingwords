Number and color words are among the very first things a kindergartener is asked to read and write. Some of these words follow the same short-vowel and phonics patterns taught in the core kindergarten sequence — like *red* and *ten*. Others don't: spelling *seven* correctly — with an e in the middle — or remembering the silent gh in *eight* takes memory more than sound-by-sound decoding.

That's exactly why this list is supplemental rather than part of the core sequence — it's useful, high-frequency vocabulary a kindergartener needs from the first week of school, but it's learned through a mix of sounding out and memorization, not through one consistent spelling rule. Practicing it builds the kind of automatic recognition that lets a child focus on what they're writing, not how to spell the color of the sky.
