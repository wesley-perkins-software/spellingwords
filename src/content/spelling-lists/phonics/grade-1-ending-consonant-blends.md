These words sample varied final blends, including **nd**, **st**, **mp**, **lk**, **lt**, **nk**, **sk**, **ft**, **ld**, and **lp**. The variety matters because the final consonants are not equally easy to hear in *hand*, *desk*, *gift*, and *help*. In every word, both consonant sounds belong to the conventional spelling even when one is less prominent in a child's pronunciation.

This lesson moves the blend work from the beginning to the end of the word. Children can reuse the same left-to-right mapping habit, but they must keep listening after the vowel instead of stopping after the first final consonant. Pronunciations vary across dialects, so the goal is not to change a child's accent; it is to connect the spoken word they know with its conventional written form.

### How to practice this list

Say a word naturally, then have your child repeat it and stretch the ending without inserting an extra vowel: *han-d*, *gif-t*, or *hel-p*. Ask the child to write the word and check the final two consonant spellings while reading it back. Begin with endings the child hears clearly, then mix the list and compare a difficult word with a known word sharing its final blend.

Writing *han* for *hand* or *gif* for *gift* is a normal attempt when the last consonant is hard to hear. Confirm the part the child represented, gently make the complete spelling visible, and let the child repair it. If the child consistently cannot identify two sounds even when the ending is stretched, return briefly to oral segmenting before asking for the written word.

When your child can spell these words in mixed order, include both final consonants with growing independence, and use stretching or a comparison word to repair an occasional omission, they are ready for Long Vowels with Silent e (VCe).
