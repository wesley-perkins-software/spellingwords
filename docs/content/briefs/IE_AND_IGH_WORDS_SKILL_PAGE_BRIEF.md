# IE and IGH Words — Approved Skill Page Content Brief

**Approval status:** Approved for drafting on 2026-08-04 after an internal acceptance-checklist review against `CANONICAL_SKILL_PAGE_STANDARD.md` §20.

- **Canonical title:** IE and IGH Words
- **Canonical id / slug:** `ie-and-igh-words`
- **Skill family:** Vowel Teams, in the frozen slot after OU and OW Words and before OO Words
- **Instructional variant:** Variant 1 — sound–spelling pattern
- **Primary audience and intent:** An adult helping a child decide when long I is spelled `ie` or `igh`.
- **Learner competency:** The learner recognizes final `ie` in a small group of short base words and `igh` within a word or at a syllable end, without assuming that every long-I word uses either spelling.
- **Conceptual scope:** Long-I `ie` in base words such as *pie* and *tie* and long-I `igh` in words such as *high* and *night*. The direct answer must say that hearing long I does not determine its spelling.
- **Out of scope:** long-I silent e (*time, ride*), final-y long I (*my, fly*), and long-E `ie` (*field, chief, piece*). These appear only as contrasts. Derived forms such as *cried* and *tried* are cautions: their `ie` results when final `y` changes before `-ed`, not from copying a final-`ie` base word.
- **Demonstration set:** `pie`, `tie`, `high`, `night`, `light`, `right`, `sight`, `bright`. This deliberately gives the more productive `igh` spelling more space. *Lie* and *die* are valid but add no new instructional condition; *sigh*, *might*, and *flight* belong in the body system rather than padding the demonstration.
- **Instructional example system:** a final-`ie` base-word group (*pie, tie, lie, die*); an `igh` syllable-end group (*high, sigh*); an `igh`-before-`t` family (*light, night, right, might, sight, bright, flight*); and separate contrast rows for VCe, final y, long-E `ie`, and derived `-ied` forms.
- **Level 1 distinction:** One sound has several spellings; inspect the written pattern and word structure rather than selecting a spelling by sound alone.
- **Level 2 cautions:** `ie` also spells long E, and final `-ied` can be created by a suffix change. Neither is part of the core pattern.
- **Level 3 nuance:** `igh` is a three-letter spelling in which `gh` is not pronounced separately. No historical explanation is needed for instruction.
- **Teaching routine:** Say the word, identify long I, sort it by visible word shape (short final `ie`, `igh`, VCe, or final y), write it, and reread it.
- **Diagnostic response:** If the child confuses the sound, contrast a long-I word with a short-I word. If the sound is secure but the spelling is not, return to a two-column `ie`/`igh` sort; if *cried* is the problem, restore *cry* and add `-ed` explicitly.
- **Signs of security:** The learner sorts new examples accurately, retains all three letters of `igh`, distinguishes *pie* from *cried*, and checks a known word family rather than inventing an absolute rule.
- **Canonical relationships:** `silent-e-long-i` only. Both pages spell the same sound through visibly different patterns, and the body explains the contrast. Same-family membership does not earn links to unrelated vowel-team pages. `nextLists` remains empty.
- **FAQ candidates:** “Is IE always long I?” (essential scope boundary); “Why does cried end in IE?” (common morphology confusion); “Are GH letters sounded in night?” (hearing/spelling diagnostic). Each adds information not duplicated by the main routine.
- **Claims requiring verification:** The positional wording must remain a tendency, not a universal rule; pronunciations and the long-E counterexamples must be dictionary-consistent; the `cry → cried` and `try → tried` explanation must match the canonical suffix-change rule.
- **Curriculum placement:** No existing canonical Grade Unit actually teaches `ie`/`igh`. Do not fabricate a reverse `skillIds` link. Record this as a Phase 2 placement gap; the Skill template already omits the placement section when reverse lookup is empty.
- **Metadata / structured data:** Use the exact canonical title and a natural description. Existing Breadcrumb and FAQ schema are sufficient; no schema implementation is implied.
- **Duplication risk:** The Long I Silent E Skill and Grade 1 final-y unit are contrasts, not content sources. Do not reproduce either lesson or its practice list.
- **Visual opportunity:** The four-way long-I comparison could become a card or diagram in a later design phase; a Markdown table is sufficient now.
