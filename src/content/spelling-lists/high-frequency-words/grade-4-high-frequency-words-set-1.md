### Use meaningful structure when it truly helps

Several longer words can be retrieved through genuine word structure. *Understand* joins **under** and **stand**. *Happened* keeps the base **happen**, including double **p**, before **-ed**. *Known* is related to **know** and keeps every letter of that word before its final **n**. These connections protect letters that may be difficult to recover from ordinary pronunciation alone.

The endings in *important* and *possible* are also stable spelling anchors. Keep **-ant** at the end of *important* and **-ible** at the end of *possible*, even when the ending vowel is not clearly heard. These are useful endings here, but the beginnings are best secured as the written syllables **im-por-** and **pos-s-** rather than forced into smaller meaningful parts.

### Protect vowels with written syllables and letter sequences

In *example*, the middle vowel may sound weak, but the written syllables **ex-am-ple** preserve **a**. The words *general* and *several* share the ending **eral**; write **gen-er-al** and **sev-er-al**, keeping both vowels even when speech makes one less distinct. *Among* can be held as **a-mong**, with **a** at the beginning and the familiar **ong** sequence at the end.

The remaining spellings need their own precise anchors. *Straight* uses **aigh**, *idea* ends in two separately heard vowels, and *quite* keeps a final **e** that distinguishes it from *quit*. Their notes isolate the part worth extra attention rather than treating each whole word as unpredictable.
