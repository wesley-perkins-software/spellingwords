These twelve words form three word families built on the short-i sound: pig/dig/fig/wig share the -ig ending, sit/hit/fit/pit share -it, and pin/win/tin/fin share -in. As with short-a, every word in a family rhymes and is spelled the same way after its first letter.

Short-i is introduced right after short-a because the two sounds are easy to tell apart by ear — a useful contrast before trickier vowel pairs come later in the sequence. Say each word slowly and listen for the short "ih" sound in the middle.

Once these three families feel solid, Short-o CVC Words is the natural next step in the vowel-by-vowel sequence.
