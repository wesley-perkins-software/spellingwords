The short "o" sound — the round, open /o/ in the middle of *hot* and *dog* — is one of the five short-vowel sounds a beginning speller works through. Like the other short vowels, most short-o words a child meets are simple consonant-vowel-consonant (CVC) patterns, so the same sound-by-sound approach applies.

**How to hear it.** Say *hot* slowly and stretch it out: *h... o... t*. The middle sound is short "o." A child who can pull a spoken word apart into its sounds this way has everything needed to spell it — write the letter for each sound, in order.

**A simple practice tip.** Say the word aloud, then stretch out the sounds together — *n... o... d* — before writing or typing it. If a word comes out wrong, read back exactly what was written and let your child listen for which sound needs fixing.

**What trips children up.** Most words here are fully regular, but *rock* ends in "ck" rather than a single "k" — two letters spelling one sound. Naming that pattern once, rather than treating it as a spelling to memorize, helps it register as a dependable rule a child will meet again in other short-vowel words.
