Dolch First Grade words pick up where the Primer tier leaves off, gathering the next set of high-frequency words a student is expected to read and spell automatically by the end of first grade. These words appear constantly in early chapter books and classroom writing, so recognizing them on sight — without sounding them out — frees a young reader's attention for the meaning of what they're reading.

Why these words matter: many of the words on this list, such as *could*, *every*, *thank*, and *were*, don't follow simple, predictable spelling patterns, which is exactly why they are taught as sight words rather than left for a child to sound out letter by letter. Practicing them now builds the kind of instant recognition that lets a first grader read smoothly, write with more confidence, and spend less effort decoding the small connector words that hold every sentence together.
