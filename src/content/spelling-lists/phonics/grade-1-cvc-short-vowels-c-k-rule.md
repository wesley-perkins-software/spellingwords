## Introduction: what students learn

This lesson reviews short-vowel CVC words and adds an important first-grade spelling choice: how to spell the /k/ sound with **c**, **k**, or **ck**.

Students practice listening for the short vowel first. Then they decide whether the /k/ sound comes at the beginning or end of the word.

## Learning Objectives

Students will learn to:

- spell simple short-vowel CVC words with more independence;
- hear the /k/ sound at the beginning or end of a word;
- use **c** before **a**, **o**, or **u** in many short words;
- use **k** before **e** or **i** in many short words;
- use **ck** after a short vowel at the end of a one-syllable word.

## Why this spelling concept matters

First graders often know the /k/ sound but are unsure which spelling to choose. This lesson gives them a practical decision path instead of asking them to memorize every word one at a time.

The C/K/CK pattern also prepares students for later spelling rules, including silent-e words and short-vowel ending patterns.

## Parent-friendly explanation

The /k/ sound can be spelled more than one way. In many short words, **c** comes before **a**, **o**, or **u**, as in *cat*, *cot*, and *cup*. The letter **k** often comes before **e** or **i**, as in *Ken* or *kit*. At the end of a short-vowel word, students often use **ck**, as in *back*, *neck*, and *duck*.

## The spelling rule or generalization

Use this first-grade generalization:

- At the beginning of a short word, use **c** before **a**, **o**, or **u**.
- At the beginning of a short word, use **k** before **e** or **i**.
- At the end of a one-syllable word right after a short vowel, use **ck**.

This is a generalization, so students will meet exceptions later. For now, it gives them a strong spelling habit.

## Common mistakes children make

- Writing **k** before every vowel because it sounds like /k/.
- Forgetting that **ck** comes after a short vowel at the end of the word.
- Spelling *duck* as *duk* or *duc*.
- Mixing up short vowel sounds before choosing the ending.

## Common Misconceptions

- **Misconception:** The letter **c** always says /k/.  
  **Correction:** The letter **c** often says /k/ before **a**, **o**, or **u**, but it can say /s/ before **e**, **i**, or **y** in many words.

- **Misconception:** Students should memorize c, k, and ck words as sight words.  
  **Correction:** Most words in this lesson are pattern-based and can be spelled by listening to sounds and applying the generalization.

## How parents can practice at home

1. Say the word slowly.
2. Ask your child to tap the sounds.
3. Ask where the /k/ sound is: beginning or end?
4. If it is at the beginning, ask what vowel comes next.
5. If it is at the end after a short vowel, remind your child to try **ck**.

Keep practice short. A few well-spelled words are better than a long list rushed through quickly.
