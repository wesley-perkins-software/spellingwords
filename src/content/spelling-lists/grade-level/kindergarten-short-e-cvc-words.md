These twelve words form three word families built on the short-e sound: bed/fed/led/wed share the -ed ending, hen/den/pen/men share -en, and net/wet/jet/vet share -et. As with every list in this sequence, every word in a family rhymes and is spelled the same way after its first letter.

Short-e is taught last among the five vowels because it's the one kindergarteners most often confuse with short-i — saving it for last means it's introduced once every other vowel is already secure, in the freshest, most-reinforced position.

With all five short vowels now complete, qu- Words is the next step — a small, rule-based extension of the same CVC pattern.
