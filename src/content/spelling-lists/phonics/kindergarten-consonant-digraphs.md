Every word so far in this sequence has matched one letter to one sound. Digraphs are the first exception: two letters, chosen together, spelling a single new sound. In "chip," the c and h are not two sounds — together they make the one "ch" sound.

This page introduces the four most common consonant digraphs — ch, sh, th, and wh — with two example words each, just enough to make the pattern noticeable. A kindergartener does not need to master every digraph word in the language here; the goal is simply recognizing that "two letters, one sound" is a real and common thing English spelling does, before Grade 1 studies each digraph in much greater depth.
