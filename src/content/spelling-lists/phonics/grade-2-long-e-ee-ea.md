## Why these words?

Every word uses `ee` or `ea` to spell long e. The list begins with familiar `ee` words, then moves to `ea` words so children can compare two spellings for the same sound. Short anchors such as _tree_ and _eat_ establish each pattern before blends and digraphs appear in _sleep_, _clean_, _dream_, _beach_, and _speak_.

This unit opens Grade 2 Core Spelling because it extends the long-vowel work from Grade 1 before the sequence moves into long-i patterns and then less familiar vowel sounds. There is no dependable sound-based rule for choosing `ee` or `ea`, so the practice goal is to remember the spelling of useful words and notice related word families.

## How to practice this list

Say a word, have the learner identify long e, and then sort it under `ee` or `ea` before spelling it. Mix the two groups after each spelling is familiar, and ask the learner to name a known word with the same vowel team when checking an answer.

The learner is ready to continue when they can keep both vowel letters in each word, sort familiar examples without relying on a position rule, and use a known word such as _keep_ or _team_ to check another spelling.
