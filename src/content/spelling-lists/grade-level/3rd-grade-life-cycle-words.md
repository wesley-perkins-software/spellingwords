### Notice doubled consonants

*Egg* ends with two *g*s, while the middle of *caterpillar* has two *l*s. The doubled letters occur in different places, so it helps to hold each one with its word rather than treating them as one spelling rule.

### Use meaningful parts and syllable chunks

The compound *tadpole* joins *tad + pole*, keeping the silent *e* in *pole*. The longer words can be organized as *lar-va*, *pu-pa*, *co-coon*, and *cat-er-pil-lar*. The repeated *co* at the start of *cocoon* leads into the vowel team *oo*.

### Compare the consonant spellings

The letter *c* spells its hard sound at the beginning of *cocoon*, but it spells its soft sound at the beginning of *cycle*. In *hatch*, the three-letter spelling *tch* comes after the short *a* sound. These whole-word comparisons keep three different consonant spellings distinct.
