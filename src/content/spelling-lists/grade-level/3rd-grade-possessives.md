An apostrophe can do two different jobs: it can show that letters are missing, as in a contraction, or it can show ownership, as in a possessive. This list focuses on the second job.

Add **'s** to a singular noun to show that one person or thing owns something: the *dog's* bone, the *teacher's* desk, the *boy's* jacket. When a plural noun already ends in *s*, add only an apostrophe after it: the *dogs'* bones, the *teachers'* desks, the *boys'* jackets. Words that change from *y* to *i* before a plural, like *family* to *families*, follow the same pattern once they are already plural: *families'*. Irregular plurals that do not end in *s*, like *children*, still take *'s*: *children's*. And singular nouns that already end in *s*, like *class*, usually still add *'s*: *class's*.
