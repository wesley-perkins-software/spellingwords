### Compare stable vowel spellings

The words *early* and *heard* both use **ear** for the vowel sound heard before **r**. Their endings distinguish them: **-ly** in *early* and **d** in *heard*. By contrast, *even* and *ever* begin with the same three letters, **eve**, but their first vowels do not sound alike. Keeping the shared written opening while listening for the whole word helps separate the pair.

### Use bases, endings, and syllable anchors

Several words can be organized with familiar structure. *Become* keeps the complete word **come** after **be**. *Later* keeps **late** and replaces its final **e** when **-er** is added. The word *able* ends with the stable spelling **-able**, while *ever* can be held in the two syllables **ev-er**.

Other words need more precise anchors. The notes isolate the unexpected vowel teams in *against* and *built*, the final **ice** in *practice*, double **f** in *difficult*, and the four-syllable structure of *especially*.
