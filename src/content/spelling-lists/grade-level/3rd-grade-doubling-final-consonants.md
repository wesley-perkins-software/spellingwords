Some short-vowel base words double the final consonant before a suffix. The doubled consonant helps keep the vowel sound short: *run* becomes *running*, *sit* becomes *sitting*, and *stop* becomes *stopped*.

This rule usually happens when a one-syllable word ends with one vowel followed by one consonant and the suffix begins with a vowel. Third graders do not need to memorize every exception yet, but they should learn to look at the base word before adding *-ing*, *-ed*, *-er*, or *-est*.
