### Compare two long spellings

*Numerator* and *denominator* share the final written sequence *ator*. Organize them as *nu-mer-a-tor* and *de-nom-i-na-tor*. The different openings—*numer* and *denomin*—carry most of the letter-order work, while the ending stays stable.

### Track c and t in the middle

In *decimal* and *percent*, *c* comes before *i* or *e* and has its soft sound. *Fraction* uses *c* before *t*, followed by the ending *tion*. *Ratio* also contains *tio*, but it ends in *o* rather than the final *n* of *fraction*. These contrasts keep similar letter sequences attached to the correct whole word.

### Use endings and meaningful parts

*Equivalent* ends in *ent*, while *percent* ends in *cent*; check the extra *c* in the latter ending. *Hundredths* can be built from *hundred + th + s*, preserving the written base before its two endings. This structure helps retain the dense final sequence *dths*.
