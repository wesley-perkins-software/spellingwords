# SpellingWords.app Visual Redesign Brief

This is a companion brief for the upcoming visual redesign of spellingwords.app. It pairs with `.claude/skills/frontend-design/SKILL.md` — that skill is the reusable design methodology; this document is the project-specific context to apply it to. It does not choose a final visual direction. It exists so a redesign session doesn't have to rediscover this context from scratch.

This is primarily a **visual** redesign. It does not authorize changes to information architecture, curriculum, routes, or established product journeys unless explicitly requested elsewhere.

## Audience

- Kindergarten through Grade 5 students (the primary users of the practice experience);
- parents;
- teachers.

## Desired character

Polished, trustworthy, warm, calm, modern, memorable, focused, highly usable. Age-appropriate without feeling babyish, and credible to the adults who choose and supervise the site.

## Product philosophy

Focused spelling practice, not attention capture. The redesign must not introduce points, timers, streaks, leaderboards, competition, casino-style reward loops, gratuitous celebration, or unnecessary account/login pressure.

## Established product structure to preserve

- custom-word practice (paste or type your own list);
- the K-5 grade-level curriculum;
- High-Frequency Words lists;
- Skill browsing and Skill practice;
- the shared practice/session experience used across entry points.

Curriculum, taxonomy, URLs, content architecture, and major user journeys are not in scope for this redesign unless explicitly authorized separately.

## Content characteristics

The design must work for both interactive practice and long-form reference content: instructional tables, curriculum lists, word inventories, FAQs, internal/related-content links, and calls to practice. Don't art-direct semantic, crawlable educational content out of the document — headings, real text, and internal links need to survive any visual treatment.

## Accessibility

WCAG 2.2 AA is the floor. Because students are primary users, favor especially clear, predictable interaction and generously sized primary controls.

## Performance

This is a frequently visited, search-discovered educational site. Protect Core Web Vitals; visual ambition should not come at the cost of load speed or stability.

## Creative freedom

This brief does not choose the final design. Fonts, colors, radii, illustration style, layout, and surface treatment are all open for the exploration phase — do not freeze any of them here.

**On the existing `docs/DESIGN_SYSTEM.md` and `docs/CURRENT_UI_AUDIT.md`:** these documents describe the site's current "Approved design direction" (a workbook aesthetic) and a gap analysis against it. Treat them as important historical/existing design inputs to read during the redesign's audit phase — not as constraints this brief is reaffirming. The current visual aesthetic is explicitly open to reconsideration; the redesign is free to propose genuinely different visual directions, including ones that depart from the workbook metaphor entirely. What must be preserved is the product structure and functionality described above, not the current visual language. This brief does not modify `docs/DESIGN_SYSTEM.md` or `docs/CURRENT_UI_AUDIT.md` — any update to those documents is a decision for the redesign phase itself.

## Avoid defaulting to

Preschool visual language; stereotypical "kids app" primary colors and cartoon styling; sterile enterprise-dashboard aesthetics; generic SaaS landing-page composition; excessive cards; arbitrary pills; generic AI gradients/glows; needless glass/grain/mesh effects; novelty typography that damages reading; unusual navigation adopted just to be distinctive; motion-heavy page entrances.

These are warnings against defaulting without a reason — not permanent bans on any of these techniques.

## Design objective

> This clearly was not generated from a generic template.

and

> This is an exceptionally well-designed real educational product.

Both, at once.
