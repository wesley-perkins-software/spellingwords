### Use the sounds that work as expected

In *am*, *at*, *can*, *in*, and *it*, a child can say the word slowly, listen from beginning to end, and match the sounds to familiar letters. The word *and* works the same way, but its final two sounds are close together: stretch the ending just enough to hear both **n** and **d**.

These six words are good evidence that high-frequency words are not automatically unusual. Encourage the child to listen and build each spelling with letters rather than memorize the word as a shape.

### Notice one extra detail in four words

The one-letter words *a* and *I* need attention for different reasons: *a* can sound different in a sentence, while *I* must be capitalized. In *is*, the final **s** represents the /z/ sound. In *the*, **th** works as a pair and the final **e** stays in the spelling even when the word sounds like “thuh.” The notes below turn each detail into a specific reminder.
