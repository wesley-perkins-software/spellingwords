### Use shared letter sequences

The words *any* and *many* share the ending **any**, even though its vowel does not have the usual short-a sound. The words *could* and *would* share **ould**: the **l** is silent, but it stays between **u** and **d** in both spellings. Learning each shared sequence once helps hold two words together.

The words *there* and *their* sound alike in many voices and both begin with **th**, so sound alone cannot choose the spelling. Their notes connect each written form with its meaning and show the different letters that follow **th**.

### Keep longer words in useful chunks

Several words can be spelled in parts without losing their letter order. In *going*, keep the base word **go** and add **ing**. The words *again*, *every*, and *people* also have useful written chunks, while their notes mark letters that may be easy to miss. In *first*, the r-controlled vowel is spelled **ir**. These known structures leave only a few specific details to secure.
