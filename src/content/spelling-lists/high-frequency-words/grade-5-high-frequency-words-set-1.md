### Use bases, endings, and related forms

Several long words become easier to retrieve when their genuine structure is visible. *Government* keeps **govern** before **ment**, and *information* keeps **inform** before **ation**. The words *attention* and *beautiful* benefit from related forms: **attentive** protects the double **t** in *attention*, while **beauty** protects **beau** in *beautiful*. Their notes capture the less obvious letters at the joins.

The endings also provide stable anchors. *Special* ends with **cial**, *future* with **ture**, and *increase* with **crease**. In *control*, the written syllables **con-trol** preserve one **n** and one **l**. These structures apply directly to the words in this set without requiring every written chunk to be a meaningful part.

### Preserve vowels that speech may not reveal clearly

Ordinary pronunciation does not always make every vowel easy to recover. The written syllables **lan-guage** keep **u** and **a** in order, while **bus-i-ness** preserves the middle **i**. In *interest*, keeping **in-ter-est** protects the second **e** even when the spoken word sounds shorter.

Two remaining words have useful vowel anchors. *Although* begins with **al** and ends with the stable **though** spelling. *Increase* keeps **ea** inside **crease**. The individual notes isolate the spellings that need the closest attention rather than treating every long word as equally difficult.
