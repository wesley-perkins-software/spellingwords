Every word in this short, final list is one a kindergartener already hears and reads constantly — but each one has a piece that does not spell out the way the rules from earlier in this sequence would predict. In "said," the middle sound is not a plain short a or short e. In "they," the "ey" ending does not follow a pattern taught anywhere else on this list.

That is exactly what makes a word a "heart word": common enough to need early practice, irregular enough that one part simply has to be memorized. Learning to spot which part of a word is regular and which part must be learned by heart is itself a useful, transferable skill — one a child will keep using well past this particular set of eight words.
