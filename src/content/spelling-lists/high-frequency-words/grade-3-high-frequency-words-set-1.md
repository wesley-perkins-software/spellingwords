### Build longer words from recognizable parts

Several words preserve smaller words inside them. *Always* joins **all** and **ways**; *everyone* joins **every** and **one**; *something* joins **some** and **thing**; and *without* joins **with** and **out**. In *another*, the familiar words **an** and **other** remain visible. Using these parts protects the letter order in words that are too long to spell reliably as one unbroken string.

The plural *children* uses a different kind of structure: it keeps the base **child** and adds **ren**. Its note shows why the written base is useful even when the spoken vowel changes.

### Keep sound-alike chunks tied to the right word

The ending **ough** does not represent the same sounds in *enough* and *through*. In *enough*, write **e-n** before **ough** and finish with the **f** sound; in *through*, write **thr** before **ough**. Treating **ough** as a stable written chunk while attaching it to the correct beginning makes the contrast easier to retrieve.

The remaining longer spellings need different supports rather than one shared rule. The notes use a less expected vowel in *friend*, syllable structure in *family*, a base plus ending in *different*, and written chunks in *question*.
