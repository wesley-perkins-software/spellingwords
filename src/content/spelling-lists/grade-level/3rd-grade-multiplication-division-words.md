### Connect *multiply* and *multiple*

*Multiply* and *multiple* share the stable opening *multip*. Their endings separate them: *multiply* finishes with *ly*, while *multiple* finishes with the written chunk *le*. Using the shared letters first reduces how much of each spelling must be remembered separately.

### Organize the longer spellings

Syllables make *multiply*, *multiple*, and *equation* easier to hold in order: *mul-ti-ply*, *mul-ti-ple*, and *e-qua-tion*. For *quotient*, use the stable written chunks *quo*, *ti*, and *ent* rather than treating those chunks as three spoken syllables. Both *quotient* and *equation* contain *qu*, but their endings are different: *quotient* ends in *ent*, while *equation* ends in *tion*.

### Compare the remaining patterns

*Divide* uses a final silent *e*, while *product* can be held as *prod-uct* and *factor* as *fac-tor*. In *array*, the beginning has one *r* and the middle has another: *ar-ray*. Keeping the two syllables visible helps preserve both *r*s and the final *ay* vowel team.
