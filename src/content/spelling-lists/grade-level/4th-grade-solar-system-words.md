*Astronaut, asteroid, meteor* — three space words with three different vowel sequences to keep straight, which is the real work behind this ten-word set, not a lesson in astronomy.

### Organize the longest words

Use syllables to hold *astronaut* as *as-tro-naut* and *satellite* as *sat-el-lite*. The shorter *asteroid* can be organized as *as-ter-oid*. These divisions keep the letter order visible, especially where *astronaut* ends in *naut*, *satellite* has doubled *l*, and *asteroid* ends in *oid*.

### Compare internal vowel sequences

Three words have memorable two-vowel sequences: *astronaut* contains *au*, *asteroid* contains *oi*, and *meteor* contains *eo*. They occur in different positions, so attach each sequence to its whole word rather than treating them as interchangeable spellings. *Eclipse* presents a different check: it begins and ends with *e*.

### Use stable chunks in the remaining words

*Planet*, *orbit*, and *comet* each have two syllables. *Planet* and *comet* share the final chunk *et*, while *orbit* ends in *it*. *Gravity* and *galaxy* both begin with *g* and end in *y*, but their middles separate them: *grav-i-ty* and *gal-ax-y*. Comparing the shared frame with the different internal letters prevents the two spellings from blending together.
