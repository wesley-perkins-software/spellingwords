### Compare two related spelling families

The words *thought* and *brought* share the ending **ought**. Keep that whole sequence after **th** in *thought* and after **br** in *brought*. The rhyming word *caught* uses **aught** instead, so its written family has **a** where the other two have **o**. Comparing **ought** with **aught** isolates the one vowel letter that changes.

### Use parts and consonant anchors

Several spellings can be organized around recognizable parts. *Toward* combines **to** with the ending **ward**, and *below* keeps **low** after **be**. The words *above* and *also* can be held in the written chunks **a-bove** and **al-so** without pretending that every chunk is a meaningful word part.

Doubled consonants anchor two other words: *across* ends in **cross** with double **s**, and *better* has double **t** between its two vowel spellings. The notes focus on the stable **t** in *often*, the meaningful prefix and stable chunk in *subject*, and the one-**l** opening of *already*.
