Longer words are spelled the same way they're read: one syllable at a time. This Skill covers four syllable types children meet across the curriculum. **Closed syllables** end in a consonant with a short vowel sound, as in the first half of *rabbit*. **Open syllables** end in a vowel with a long vowel sound, as in *paper*. **R-controlled syllables** carry the same ar/or/er/ir/ur patterns taught in one-syllable words, as in *winter*. **The final stable -le syllable** has no separate vowel sound of its own — the *l* carries it, as in *little* and *table*.
