A prefix is a meaningful word part added before a base word or root. This list helps third graders build longer words while keeping the meaningful spelling parts visible.

The list begins with five *un-* words and four *re-* words built on familiar bases: *fair, happy, kind, safe, lock, do, play, read,* and *write*. The spelling of each base stays recognizable when the prefix is added. *Preview* and *predict* introduce *pre-* (“before”), while the *dis-* and *mis-* words require meaning as well as sound: *dis-* can signal “not” or a reversal, and *mis-* often signals an error, as in *misread* and *misspell*. In *discover* and *dishonest*, the whole word's present meaning is less easily worked out one part at a time, so the prefix is still a useful spelling chunk without being a complete definition.

This lesson follows Grade 2 work with contractions, where learners accounted for meaningful word parts and apostrophes. Here they preserve a base or root while adding something at the beginning; the next lesson applies the same word-part thinking to suffixes at the end.

## How to practice this list

Say a word, ask your child to identify its prefix, and write the remaining base when it is a familiar word: *un + safe, re + play,* or *mis + read*. Add the prefix without changing that base, then read the finished word and explain its meaning. Sort the list by prefix before mixing the groups for dictation. For *dis-* and *mis-*, give a brief sentence so meaning helps them choose the intended beginning.

A learner may omit a letter from the prefix, substitute a competing prefix, or change a base spelling that should stay stable. Treat attempts such as *uhappy* with a missing prefix letter or *disread* for *misread* as useful evidence about the uncertain part: uncover or rewrite only the prefix, compare its meaning, and let them preserve the part already spelled correctly. An error inside *write* or *honest* needs separate review and does not by itself show confusion about prefixes.

Move on when your child can identify the prefix and underlying word part, preserve familiar base spellings, and choose among these prefixes by meaning in mixed sentence dictation with only occasional prompting.
