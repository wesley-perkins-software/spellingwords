Every word on this list has two syllables, and every syllable uses a pattern a second grader already knows — the new skill is breaking the word apart, spelling each piece, and putting it back together. *Rabbit* and *basket* split into two closed syllables, each with a short vowel. *Paper* and *baby* split into an open syllable followed by a closed one, so the first syllable's vowel says its own name. *Winter*, *garden*, and *number* end in an r-controlled syllable — the same *ar*, *or*, *er*, *ir*, and *ur* patterns taught in Grade 1, now appearing at the end of a longer word instead of standing alone.

This is where Grade 1's one-syllable knowledge finally gets applied to real, everyday words. A child does not need a new vowel rule to spell *winter* — they need to recognize that *-ter* is the same r-controlled pattern they already know from words like *her*, just placed at the end of a two-syllable word.
