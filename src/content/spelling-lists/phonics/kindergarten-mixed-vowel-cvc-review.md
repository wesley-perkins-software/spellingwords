Every word in this list keeps the familiar consonant-vowel-consonant shape, but the vowel sound changes from word to word — jam, lid, cob, mud, wet, rag, dig, sub move through all five short vowels in no particular order.

That mix is the point. Spelling a whole list of short-a words in a row is a different, easier task than picking out the vowel sound in a word with no advance warning of which one it will be. This review checks the second, harder skill — and it uses words a child has not seen on any earlier list, so a good score here means the short-vowel pattern has genuinely transferred, not just been memorized one list at a time.
